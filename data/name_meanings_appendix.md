## Biblical Name Meanings Appendix

- Adam — “Man” / “of the red earth” (Hebrew adamah)
  First human formed from the ground; his name is literally what he was made from.
- Eve (Chavah) — “Living” / “life-giver” (Hebrew chayah)
  Mother of all living; the name God (and Adam) gave her after the promise of redemption.
- Cain — “Acquired” / “spear” (Hebrew qanah)
  Eve said, “I have acquired a man with the help of the LORD” — but he became the first murderer.
- Abel — “Breath” / “vanity” / “futility” (Hebrew hevel)
  His life was short and “vanished like a breath” — the first martyr.
- Seth — “Appointed” / “compensation” / “placed” (Hebrew shith)
  “God has appointed another seed instead of Abel” — the chosen line continues through him.
- Enosh — “Man” / “mortal” / “frail” (Hebrew enosh)
  In his days “men began to call upon the name of the LORD” — highlights human weakness.
- Kenan (Cainan) — “Possession” / “smith” or “spear” (related to Cain)
  Continues the line; name echoes the “acquired” theme of the pre-flood world.
- Mahalalel — “Praise of God” / “the shining one of God” (Hebrew mahalal + El)
  His very name is a shout of praise in a darkening world.
- Jared — “Descent” / “he shall come down” (Hebrew yarad)
  Traditional Jewish interpretation: “he shall come down” — pointing to the descent of the sons of God or the coming judgment.
- Enoch — “Dedicated” / “trained” / “initiated” (Hebrew chanok)
  Walked so closely with God he was “taken” without dying — fully dedicated.
- Methuselah — “When he dies, it shall be sent” / “man of the dart” (Hebrew muth + shelach)
  His death coincided with the Flood — his name was a 969-year prophecy of judgment.
- Lamech — “Powerful” / “overthrower” (or possibly “poor” / “despairing”)
  Father of Noah; spoke of relief from the curse — but his line produced violence.
- Noah — “Rest” / “comfort” / “relief” (Hebrew nuach)
  “This one will bring us relief from our work and the toil of our hands” — the ark brought rest from judgment.
- Shem — “Name” / “renown” (Hebrew shem)
  Ancestor of the Semitic line; through him came the “Name” (the line that would bear God’s name).
- Japheth — “Enlarged” / “may he enlarge” (Hebrew yaphath)
  “May God enlarge Japheth” — his descendants spread across the earth.
- Ishmael — “God hears” (Hebrew Yishmaʿel)
  God heard Hagar’s affliction in the wilderness.
- Esau — “Hairy” / “red” (Hebrew ʿEsav)
  Born hairy and red; sold his birthright for red stew.
- Jacob — “Supplanter” / “heel-grabber” (Hebrew Yaʿaqov)
  Grabbed Esau’s heel at birth; later supplanted him and became Israel.
- Israel — “God contends” / “strives with God” (Hebrew Yisraʾel)
  Wrestled with God and prevailed; name of the covenant nation.
- Reuben — “Behold a son” (Hebrew Reʾuven)
  Leah’s first son; “Behold, a son!” — hoped it would win Jacob’s love.
- Simeon — “Heard” (Hebrew Shimʿon)
  “The LORD has heard that I am hated” — Leah’s second son.
- Levi — “Joined” / “attached” (Hebrew Levi)
  “Now my husband will be joined to me” — tribe of priests.
- Judah — “Praise” (Hebrew Yehudah)
  “This time I will praise the LORD” — royal tribe and ancestor of Messiah.
- Joseph — “He adds” / “may He add” (Hebrew Yosef)
  God added to his blessings despite betrayal; savior of his family.
- Benjamin — “Son of the right hand” (Hebrew Binyamin)
  Rachel’s last son; “son of my sorrow” became “son of the right hand.”
- Moses — “Drawn out” (of water) (Hebrew Mosheh)
  Drawn out of the Nile; drew Israel out of Egypt.
- Aaron — “Exalted” / “mountain of strength” / “teacher” (Hebrew Aharon)
  First high priest; exalted to lead worship.
- Joshua (Yehoshua) — “Yahweh saves” (Hebrew Yehoshuaʿ)
  Led Israel into the Promised Land; name points to the ultimate Savior.
- Caleb — “Whole-hearted” / “dog” (faithful like a dog) (Hebrew Kalev)
  Followed the LORD fully; entered the land.
- Rahab — “Broad” / “spacious” (Hebrew Rachav)
  God’s mercy was broad enough to save a Canaanite prostitute.
- Ruth — “Friend” / “companion” (Hebrew Ruth)
  Loyal friend to Naomi; became ancestor of David and Messiah.
- Samuel — “Heard of God” / “God has heard” (Hebrew Shmuʾel)
  God heard Hannah’s prayer; last judge and anointer of kings.
- Saul — “Asked for” / “prayed for” (Hebrew Shaʾul)
  The people “asked for” a king; first king of Israel.
- David — “Beloved” (Hebrew Dawid)
  The beloved king; “a man after God’s own heart.”
- Solomon — “Peaceable” / “peaceful” (Hebrew Shelomoh)
  Brought peace and built the Temple.
- Elijah — “My God is Yahweh” (Hebrew Eliyahu)
  Confronted Baal worship; “My God is Yahweh.”
- Elisha — “My God is salvation” (Hebrew Elishaʿ)
  Continued Elijah’s work; brought salvation and healing.
- Isaiah — “Yahweh saves” (Hebrew Yeshaʿyahu)
  Prophesied the coming Savior who would save His people.
- Jeremiah — “Yahweh exalts” / “Yahweh throws” (Hebrew Yirmeyahu)
  Exalted God even while weeping over Jerusalem.
- Ezekiel — “God strengthens” (Hebrew Yehezqel)
  Strengthened by God during exile.
- Daniel — “God is my judge” (Hebrew Daniyyel)
  Stood before earthly kings; God was his true Judge.
- Hosea — “Salvation” (Hebrew Hosheaʿ)
  His life pictured God’s faithful salvation of unfaithful Israel.
- Jonah — “Dove” (Hebrew Yonah)
  Symbol of peace; fled yet brought salvation to Nineveh.
- Micah — “Who is like Yahweh?” (Hebrew Mikah)
  Asked the rhetorical question that points to God’s uniqueness.
- Nahum — “Comfort” (Hebrew Nachum)
  Brought comfort by announcing the end of Nineveh’s oppression.
- Habakkuk — “Embrace” (Hebrew Chavaqquq)
  Embraced God in faith despite coming judgment.
- Zephaniah — “Yahweh has hidden” / “protected” (Hebrew Tsephanyah)
  Hidden and protected the remnant during judgment.
- Haggai — “Festal” / “my feasts” (Hebrew Chaggay)
  Urged the people to rebuild the Temple for God’s festivals.
- Zechariah — “Yahweh remembers” (Hebrew Zekharyah)
  God remembered His covenant promises.
- Malachi — “My messenger” (Hebrew Malʾaki)
  The last Old Testament messenger before John the Baptist.
- Esther (Hadassah) — “Star” / “myrtle” (hidden beauty) (Hebrew Hadassah)
  Hidden like a star; saved her people.
- Mordecai — “Little man” or “pure myrrh” (humble servant) (Hebrew Mordokay)
  Humble servant who refused to bow to Haman.
- Ezra — “Help” (Hebrew ʿEzraʾ)
  Helped restore the Law and the people after exile.
- Nehemiah — “Yahweh comforts” (Hebrew Nechemyah)
  Comforted and rebuilt Jerusalem’s walls.
- Mary (Miriam) — “Bitter” / “beloved” / “exalted one” (Hebrew Miryam)
  Mother of Jesus; experienced bitterness yet exaltation.
- Joseph (NT) — “He adds” (same as OT Joseph)
  God added protection and provision for the holy family.
- John (Yochanan) — “Yahweh is gracious” (Hebrew Yochanan)
  Forerunner of Jesus; proclaimed God’s grace.
- Jesus (Yeshua) — “Yahweh saves” (Hebrew Yeshuaʿ)
  The ultimate fulfillment: “He will save His people from their sins.”
- Peter (Cephas / Petros) — “Rock” / “stone” (Aramaic/Greek)
  The rock on which the Church would be built.
- Andrew — “Manly” / “courageous” (Greek Andreas)
  Courageous fisherman turned fisher of men.
- James (Jacob) — “Supplanter” (Hebrew Yaʿaqov)
  One of the “sons of thunder”; later a pillar of the Church.
- Philip — “Lover of horses” (used for “lover of people”) (Greek Philippos)
  Brought people to Jesus (e.g., the Ethiopian eunuch).
- Bartholomew (Nathanael) — “Gift of God” (Hebrew Nethanʾel)
  Jesus called him “an Israelite in whom is no deceit.”
- Thomas — “Twin” / “doubter” (Aramaic Taʾoma)
  Doubting Thomas; later declared “My Lord and my God!”
- Matthew (Levi) — “Gift of Yahweh” (Hebrew Mattityahu)
  Tax collector given the gift of apostleship and authorship.
- Simon the Zealot — “He has heard” (Hebrew Shimʿon)
  Zealous revolutionary transformed into apostle.
- Judas (not Iscariot) — “Praise” (Hebrew Yehudah)
  Praised God; author of the epistle of Jude.
- Paul (Saul) — “Small” / “humble” (Latin Paulus)
  Changed from “asked for” (Saul) to the humble apostle to the Gentiles.
- Timothy — “Honoring God” (Greek Timotheos)
  Honored God as Paul’s faithful son in the faith.
- Titus — “Honorable” / “defender” (Latin Titus)
  Defended the faith and organized churches.
- Philemon — “Affectionate” / “kissing” (Greek Philemon)
  Showed affectionate forgiveness to his runaway slave.
- Barnabas — “Son of encouragement” (Aramaic Bar-Nabas)
  Encouraged the early Church and Paul.
- Stephen — “Crown” / “garland” (Greek Stephanos)
  Received the martyr’s crown as the first Christian martyr.
- Lydia — “From Lydia” (purple seller — royal color) (Greek Lydia)
  First European convert; “dealer in purple” (royal trade).
- Aquila — “Eagle” (Latin Aquila)
  Strong “eagle” who with Priscilla taught Apollos.
- Priscilla — “Ancient” / “venerable” (Latin Priscilla)
  Venerable teacher alongside her husband.
- Apollos — “Destroyer” or “of Apollo” (eloquent preacher) (Greek Apollos)
  Mighty preacher whose eloquence was refined by Aquila and Priscilla.
- Silas (Silvanus) — “Forest” / “wood” (steady like a tree) (Latin Silvanus)
  Steady companion who endured prison with Paul.
- Onesimus — “Useful” / “profitable” (Greek Onesimos)
  The runaway slave who became “useful” to Paul and Philemon.
- Phoebe — “Bright” / “radiant” (Greek Phoibe)
  Radiant deaconess who carried Paul’s letter to Rome.
- Gaius — “Rejoice” / “happy” (Latin Gaius)
  Hospitable host who brought joy to the Church.
- Erastus — “Beloved” (Greek Erastos)
  Beloved city treasurer who served Paul.
- Demas — “Popular” (Greek Demas)
  Loved popularity more than Christ and forsook Paul.
- Mark (John Mark) — “Hammer” or “polite” (Latin Marcus)
  Initially faltered but later became useful to Paul.
- Luke — “Light-giving” / “from Lucania” (Greek Loukas)
  “Beloved physician” who brought gospel light.
- Theophilus — “Lover of God” (Greek Theophilos)
  The one Luke wrote to — “lover of God.”
- Diotrephes — “Nurtured by Zeus” (loved preeminence) (Greek Diotrephes)
  Loved preeminence and opposed apostolic authority.
- Gamaliel — “God is my reward” (Hebrew Gamaliel)
  Wise teacher who advised the Sanhedrin wisely.
- Nicodemus — “Victory of the people” (Greek Nikodemos)
  Came to Jesus by night seeking truth.
- Lazarus — “God has helped” (Hebrew Elʿazar)
  Raised from the dead; God helped him.
- Martha — “Lady” / “mistress” (Aramaic Martha)
  Busy homemaker who learned to sit at Jesus’ feet.
- Mary Magdalene — “Of Magdala” (tower) (Hebrew Miryam)
  “Tower” of strength; first witness to the resurrection.
- Salome — “Peace” / “peaceful” (Hebrew Shalom)
  Mother of James and John; sought peaceful places at Jesus’ right hand.
- Barabbas — “Son of the father” (Aramaic Bar-Abba)
  Released in place of the true Son of the Father.
- Pilate (Pontius) — “Armed with a spear” (Latin Pilatus)
  Roman judge who washed his hands of Jesus.
- Herod — “Heroic” / “son of a hero” (Greek Herodes)
  Multiple “heroic” kings who opposed God’s plan.
- Ananias — “Yahweh has been gracious” (Hebrew Chananyah)
  Multiple figures shown God’s grace (one healed Paul, one lied).
- Sapphira — “Sapphire” (beautiful but deceptive) (Greek Sapphira)
  Beautiful exterior but deceptive heart.
- Cornelius — “Horn” (Latin Cornelius)
  Roman centurion whose “horn” (strength) was turned to God.
- Felix — “Happy” / “fortunate” (Latin Felix)
  Governor who trembled but never found true happiness.
- Festus — “Festive” / “joyful” (Latin Festus)
  Roman governor who heard Paul’s defense joyfully.
- Agrippa — “Wild horse” (Greek Agrippas)
  King who heard the gospel but remained untamed.
- Bernice — “Bringer of victory” (Greek Bernike)
  Sister of Agrippa; present at Paul’s trial.
- Artemas — “Gift of Artemis” or follower (Greek)
  Faithful coworker Paul sent to Crete.
- Tychicus — “Fortunate” / “chance” (Greek Tychikos)
  “Lucky” messenger who carried Paul’s letters.
- Epaphras — “Lovely” / “charming” (Greek Epaphras)
  Lovely intercessor who prayed fervently for Colossae.
- Archippus — “Master of the horse” (Greek Archippos)
  Called to “fulfill your ministry” like a faithful rider.
- Apphia — “Productive” / “increasing” (Greek Apphia)
  Hostess whose house church grew.
- Euodia — “Prosperous journey” / “success” (Greek Euodia)
  Worked hard in the gospel but needed unity.
- Syntyche — “With fate” / “fortunate together” (Greek Syntyche)
  Called to agree in the Lord.
- Clement — “Mild” / “merciful” (Latin Clemens)
  Fellow worker whose name is in the Book of Life.
- Epaphroditus — “Lovely” / “charming” (Greek)
  Risked his life to serve Paul.
- Onesiphorus — “Bringing profit” (Greek Onesiphoros)
  Refreshing and profitable servant.
- Aristarchus — “Best ruler” (Greek Aristarchos)
  Loyal prisoner with Paul.
- Zacchaeus — “Pure” / “innocent” (Hebrew Zakkay)
  Tax collector made pure by Jesus.
- Bartimaeus — “Son of Timaeus” / “honorable” (Aramaic)
  Blind beggar healed and followed Jesus.
- Jairus — “He enlightens” (Hebrew Yaʾir)
  Synagogue ruler whose daughter was raised.
- Simeon (NT) — “Heard”
  Held baby Jesus and declared he had “seen” salvation.
- Anna — “Grace” / “favor” (Hebrew Channah)
  Prophetess who recognized the Messiah.
- Elizabeth — “God is my oath” (Hebrew Elisheva)
  Mother of John; God kept His oath.
- Zacharias — “Yahweh remembers” (Hebrew Zekharyah)
  Father of John; God remembered him.
- John the Baptist — “Yahweh is gracious” (Hebrew Yochanan)
  Prepared the way with grace.
- Herodias — “Heroic” (feminine form)
  Manipulative “heroine” who sought John’s head.
- Cleopas — “Glory of the father” (Greek Kleopas)
  Disciple on Emmaus road who saw the risen Christ.
- Matthias — “Gift of Yahweh” (Hebrew Mattityahu)
  Chosen by lot to replace Judas.
- Philip (evangelist) — “Lover of horses/people” (Greek Philippos)
  Evangelized Samaria and the Ethiopian.
- Simon the Sorcerer — “He has heard”
  Heard the gospel but sought power.
- Dorcas (Tabitha) — “Gazelle” (Aramaic Tabitha)
  Full of good works; raised to life.
- Sergius Paulus — “Small” / “humble” (Latin)
  Proconsul who believed after seeing God’s power.
- Elymas (Bar-Jesus) — “Wise man” (sorcerer)
  Opposed the gospel.
- Jason — “Healer” (Greek Iason)
  Hosted Paul and faced riot.
- Dionysius — “Of Dionysus” (Greek)
  Areopagite who believed Paul’s message.
- Damaris — “Calf” / “gentle” (Greek)
  Athenian woman who joined the believers.
- Gallio — “Gentle” / “calm” (Latin)
  Proconsul who dismissed charges against Paul.
- Alexander the coppersmith — “Defender of men” (Greek Alexandros)
  Opposed Paul.
- Hymenaeus — “Of Hymen” (marriage god)
  False teacher who shipwrecked faith.
- Phygelus — “Fugitive” (Greek Phygelos)
  Deserted Paul in Asia.
- Hermogenes — “Born of Hermes”
  Also deserted.
- Crescens — “Growing” (Latin Crescens)
  Faithful coworker who went to Galatia.
- Pudens — “Modest” / “shamefaced” (Latin)
  Roman Christian who greeted Paul.
- Linus — “Flaxen” / “net” (Greek Linos)
  Early Church leader.
- Claudia — “Lame” (but faithful) (Latin Claudia)
  Woman who greeted Paul.
- Eubulus — “Good counselor” (Greek Euboulos)
  Faithful in Rome.
- Zenas — “Living” (Greek Zenas)
  Lawyer who traveled with Apollos.
- Jude (Judas, brother of James) — “Praise” (Hebrew Yehudah)
  Author of the epistle of Jude.
- Sosthenes — “Safe in strength” (Greek Sosthenes)
  Ruler of synagogue who believed.
- Crispus — “Curly-haired” (Latin Crispus)
  Synagogue ruler baptized by Paul.
- Fortunatus — “Fortunate” (Latin)
  Corinthian who refreshed Paul.
- Achaicus — “From Achaia” (sorrowing)
  Helped Paul.
- Stephanas — “Crown” (Greek Stephanas)
  First converts in Achaia.
- Chloe — “Green herb” / “blooming” (Greek Chloe)
  Informed Paul of divisions.
- Nymphas — “Bridegroom” (Greek Nymphas)
  Hosted a house church.
