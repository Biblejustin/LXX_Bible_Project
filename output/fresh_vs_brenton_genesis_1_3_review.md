# Fresh vs Brenton Review: Genesis 1-3

Scope:
- Fresh draft reviewed against Brenton for drafted verses only.
- Focus = language-important or theology-important differences.
- Purely stylistic differences left out.

## Changed After Review

### Genesis 1:27
- Brenton: `And God made man, according to the image of God he made him, male and female he made them.`
- Fresh now: `And God made humankind; according to God's image he made humankind. Male and female he made them.`
- Why important:
  - Greek shifts singular to plural inside one line.
  - Brenton keeps singular `man`.
  - Fresh keeps collective-human sense more clearly, but no longer uses awkward `he made it`.
- Assessment:
  - Difference matters for anthropology and generic-human language.
  - Fresh wording now cleaner than prior draft.

### Genesis 2:4
- Brenton: `This is the book of the generation of heaven and earth...`
- Fresh now: `This is the book of the origin of Heaven and Earth...`
- Why important:
  - Greek `βίβλος γενέσεως` carries origin/generation/genesis force.
  - Prior fresh draft `book of the genesis` was too stiff in English.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh now smoother.
  - Brenton still preserves the older formal register.

### Genesis 2:7
- Brenton: `...the man became a living soul.`
- Fresh now: `...the human became a living being.`
- Why important:
  - Greek `ψυχὴν ζῶσαν` can be rendered `living soul` or `living being/creature`.
  - This touches anthropology and later Pauline echo in 1 Cor 15:45.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh `living being` reads better in modern English.
  - Brenton `living soul` preserves stronger theological resonance.

### Genesis 3:1
- Brenton: `the most crafty`
- Fresh now: `the most shrewd`
- Why important:
  - Greek `φρονιμώτατος` points to intelligence/shrewdness.
  - Prior fresh `most prudent` sounded too positive.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh now better.
  - Brenton's `crafty` is darker; fresh keeps ambiguity better.

### Genesis 3:17
- Brenton: `cursed is the ground in thy labours`
- Fresh now: `the ground is cursed in your labors`
- Why important:
  - Greek `ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις σου` points to labor/work sphere.
  - Prior fresh `in your works` was too wooden.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh now better.
  - Brenton was closer here.

## Kept As Important Differences

### Genesis 1:2
- Brenton: `the Spirit of God moved over the water`
- Fresh: `a wind from God was moving over the water`
- Why important:
  - Greek `πνεῦμα Θεοῦ` can point toward wind, breath, or Spirit.
  - This is major theology-sensitive wording.
- Assessment:
  - Not settled yet.
  - Fresh keeps meteorological possibility alive.
  - Brenton preserves traditional theological reading.

### Genesis 1:5
- Brenton: `the first day`
- Fresh: `day one`
- Why important:
  - Greek uses cardinal `μία`, not ordinal.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh is more literal.
  - Brenton is smoother and more traditional.

### Genesis 2:18
- Brenton: `a help suitable to him`
- Fresh: `a helper corresponding to him`
- Why important:
  - Greek `βοηθὸν κατ᾿ αὐτόν` carries correspondence/counterpart force.
  - Impacts how relation between man and woman gets heard.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh preserves counterpart nuance better.

### Genesis 2:21-22
- Brenton: `rib` / `formed`
- Fresh: `side` / `built`
- Why important:
  - Greek `πλευρά` = side, not only rib.
  - Greek `ᾠκοδόμησεν` = built.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh is closer to Greek imagery.
  - Brenton is more traditional.

### Genesis 2:23
- Brenton: `taken out of her husband`
- Fresh: `taken from her man`
- Why important:
  - Greek keeps man/woman wordplay closer than `husband/wife` language does.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh preserves wordplay better.
  - Brenton reads more conventionally.

### Genesis 3:15
- Brenton: `watch against thy head ... watch against his heel`
- Fresh: `watch your head ... watch his heel`
- Why important:
  - Greek `τηρέω` is not the common crush/strike verb.
  - This matters for messianic reading and later doctrinal tradition.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh and Brenton both resist overreading `crush`.
  - Still major verse for user review.

### Genesis 3:16
- Brenton: `thy submission shall be to thy husband`
- Fresh: `your turning will be toward your husband`
- Why important:
  - Greek `ἀποστροφή` more directly suggests turning/orientation.
  - Traditional renderings often move toward submission/desire.
- Assessment:
  - Fresh is more cautious and more lexical.
  - Brenton is more interpretive.
